# Euro Car Parks (ECP) Appeal Guide

Euro Car Parks is one of the largest private parking operators in the UK. If you've received a Parking Charge Notice (PCN) from them, it is a private invoice, not a criminal fine.

## Common Winning Defenses against ECP

### 1. The BPA Grace Period (Section 13)
The BPA Code of Practice requires ECP to allow a "grace period" of at least 10 minutes after the parking period ends. If your PCN is for an overstay of 1-9 minutes, it is often invalid.

### 2. Signage Visibility
ECP often uses ANPR (Automatic Number Plate Recognition) to monitor entrances. However, the signs must be clear, legible, and visible even in poor lighting. If the signs are obscured by trees or poorly positioned, the "contract" was never formed.

### 3. Landowner Authority
Under the BPA Code, ECP must have written authority from the landowner to issue charges. If they cannot provide proof of this contract, they have no legal standing.

## How to Appeal Your ECP PCN

1. **Do Not Pay Yet**: Paying is an admission of liability.
2. **Gather Evidence**: Take photos of the signs and the location.
3. **Use the Online Portal**: Submit your appeal through the Euro Car Parks website.
4. **Cite the BPA Code**: Explicitly mention the specific section of the Code of Practice you believe they have breached.

## If ECP Rejects Your Appeal
If ECP rejects your initial appeal, they **must** provide you with a POPLA (Parking on Private Land Appeals) code. POPLA is an independent body, and their decisions are binding on the operator but not on you.
