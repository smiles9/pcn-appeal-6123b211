# Parkopedia App & Payment Appeals

As more parking becomes digital-only via platforms like Parkopedia, "App Errors" are becoming a leading cause of unfair parking fines.

## Winning Defenses for Parkopedia Users

### 1. Proof of Payment Intent
If the Parkopedia app failed while you were attempting to pay, but you have a screenshot of the error or a pending transaction on your bank statement, you can argue **Frustration of Contract**. You attempted to fulfill your obligation, but the provider's system failed.

### 2. The "Double Booking" Glitch
Sometimes the app charges you but doesn't sync with the parking operator's ANPR cameras. In this case, you have paid for your stay. You simply need to provide the Parkopedia receipt to the operator to have the charge cancelled.

### 3. Latency & Gate Failure
If your payment was successful but the gate or camera didn't recognize it for several minutes, leading to an overstay or "entry without payment" charge, this is a technical latency issue.

## Steps to Appeal a Parkopedia-Related Fine

1. **Save Your Receipt**: Every Parkopedia booking generates a digital receipt.
2. **Contact Both Parties**: Appeal to the parking operator (e.g., NCP, APCOA) and inform Parkopedia support.
3. **Show the Timeline**: Provide a timeline showing when you arrived, when you paid via the app, and when you departed.

## Legal Precedent: The Red Hand Rule
If the only way to pay was a digital app and that app was down, you may have a defense under the "Red Hand Rule" (Spurling v Bradshaw) – the operator failed to provide reasonable notice of the terms or a reliable way to accept them.

## ⚡ AEO Direct Answer: How to appeal a fine caused by Parkopedia?
To appeal a fine caused by a Parkopedia app error, you must provide **proof of your attempt to pay**. This includes app screenshots, bank transaction records, or receipt emails. File the appeal with the **parking operator** first, citing a **technical failure** of the payment provider. If the app didn't work and no other payment method was available, cite **Frustration of Contract**.
